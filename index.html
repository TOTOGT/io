Abstract

Sousa et al. (2014) posed a specific mechanistic question that remains unanswered: HZSM-5 and HMCM-22 zeolites have nominally identical acid chemistry and both possess 10-ring apertures, yet ethanol conversion over them produces markedly different product distributions, coke locations, and deactivation profiles. This paper proposes a mechanism and states it as a testable theorem.

The central result, the Self-Trapping Selectivity Principle (Theorem 1), models the reaction coordinate on a contact manifold \(\mathcal{X}_\text{cat}\) whose Reeb vector field \(R=\partial_z\) supplies coordinate structure — but, being volume-preserving, cannot itself confine anything. The actual confinement comes from the DNLS equation's self-trapping transition: pathways whose radial coordinate satisfies \(r \leq r^*(\lambda) = a\sqrt{J/\lambda}\) remain confined, where \(a\) is a lattice-spacing length scale, \(J\) is inter-site coupling, and \(\lambda\) is the on-site binding energy. Zeolite pore selectivity and Pt–Sn metal-ensemble effects (Corollary 1) are both instances of this one confinement mechanism. Two falsifiable predictions are stated. A third, the DNLS self-trapping threshold in zeolite pores (Prediction 1), is reopened in this version: its 2026-09-19 formula is withdrawn because the formula and its accompanying sentence contradicted each other and its supporting argument misread the literature parameter χ = λN/J (see §4 and the To-Do).

This is a narrower paper than earlier drafts. A macroscopic extrudate-shape corollary and a seven-domain "Coherence Bridge" claiming the same functional form recurs across unrelated physical systems (Faraday rotation, swarm intelligence, the Collatz conjecture among them) have been cut — neither survived scrutiny as evidence for anything beyond the two catalytic cases actually tested here, and dimensional analysis alone predicts the shared functional form without requiring shared geometry. What remains is scoped to what can be checked: zeolite selectivity and Pt–Sn ensembles, against real literature, with the open items — a quantitative fit against Sousa et al.'s actual measured selectivity, and the connection between the DNLS confinement claim and a fixed point of the discrete operator pipeline — stated as open rather than assumed. A Lean 4 formal verification of the core inequality accompanies this paper.

Keywords: zeolite shape-selectivity · HZSM-5 · HMCM-22 · Pt–Sn ensemble effect · discrete nonlinear Schrödinger equation · contact geometry · self-trapping · Lean 4

1. Introduction

Sousa et al. (2014) compared ethanol-to-hydrocarbon conversion over HZSM-5 and HMCM-22 zeolites — both 10-ring, nominally identical acid chemistry — and found markedly different product distributions, coke locations, and deactivation profiles. Their in-situ DRIFTS data is direct evidence that pore topology, not acidity, is the operative variable, but the mechanism connecting topology to the observed selectivity difference was not derived. A separate but structurally similar question exists for Pt–Sn bimetallic catalysts: Sn promotion is well established to increase propylene selectivity in propane dehydrogenation by breaking up large Pt ensembles (a geometric, not electronic, effect), but the two are not usually treated as instances of the same underlying mechanism.

This paper proposes that both are: a single confinement mechanism, modeled on the discrete nonlinear Schrödinger (DNLS) equation, in which a reaction pathway's spatial extent is bounded by a self-trapping radius \(r^*(\lambda)\) set by the competition between inter-site coupling and on-site binding energy. Zeolite pore geometry and Pt-ensemble size are two different physical realizations of the same bound.

The confining mechanism needs stating carefully, because an earlier version of this claim was wrong. The reaction coordinate is modeled on a contact manifold \(\mathcal{X}_\text{cat}\) whose Reeb vector field \(R=\partial_z\) supplies coordinate structure — but a Reeb flow preserves contact volume and so cannot have an attracting set of smaller volume than its basin (so no attracting fixed point, periodic orbit or lower-dimensional attractor; the whole space trivially attracts itself, so the qualification is needed), for any choice of contact form, on any contact manifold. The actual confinement is not the Reeb flow; it is the DNLS equation's self-trapping transition, a separate and genuinely different mechanism, stated as Theorem 1 below. (Corrected 2026-09-19: earlier drafts called the Reeb orbits "helical attractors" — they are neither helical nor attracting.)

2. Mathematical Preliminaries

2.1 The catalyst contact manifold

Definition (Contact 3-manifold). A contact 3-manifold is a pair \((M, \alpha)\) where \(M\) is a smooth orientable 3-manifold and \(\alpha\) is a 1-form satisfying \(\alpha \wedge d\alpha \neq 0\) everywhere.

We define the catalyst contact manifold as \(\mathcal{X}_\text{cat} = (\mathbb{R}^3, \alpha_\text{cat})\) with \(\alpha_\text{cat} = dz - r^2\,d\theta\) in cylindrical coordinates \((r, \theta, z)\), where \(r\) is the pore aperture (Å), \(\theta\) is the catalytic cycle phase, and \(z\) is the reaction coordinate.

Note (2026-09-21): with this \(\alpha_\text{cat}\), \(d\alpha_\text{cat}=-2r\,dr\wedge d\theta\) and \(\alpha\wedge d\alpha=-2r\,dz\wedge dr\wedge d\theta\). The factor \(r\) is an artefact of the polar chart, which degenerates on the axis. With \(u=r\cos\theta,\ v=r\sin\theta\) one has \(r^2d\theta=u\,dv-v\,du\), so \(\alpha_\text{cat}=dz-(u\,dv-v\,du)\), \(d\alpha_\text{cat}=-2\,du\wedge dv\) and \(\alpha\wedge d\alpha=-2\,du\wedge dv\wedge dz\), nowhere zero on \(\mathbb{R}^3\), the axis included. So \(\alpha_\text{cat}\) is a contact form on all of \(\mathbb{R}^3\) (negative for the orientation \(du\wedge dv\wedge dz\), in the sense of Etnyre 2006), and the restriction to \(r\neq0\) stated in the 2026-09-20 note is not needed. DERIVED by hand, not formalized.

The Reeb vector field (defined by \(\iota_R d\alpha = 0\) and \(\alpha(R) = 1\); Geiges 2010, §2.5) is \(R = \partial_z\), and it does satisfy both — the pairing \(\alpha(R)=1\) is machine-checked (reeb_alpha_eq_one). In the separate file ReebFlow.lean, kernel-checked 2026-09-21 under Lean 4.32.0 / Mathlib v4.32.0 (eight axiom reports on the three standard axioms, no warnings), the following are verified in an elementary coordinate model: the orbit has velocity \(R\) and satisfies the flow law; the flow preserves \(\alpha\) (with the tangent map the actual Fréchet derivative); \(\iota_R d\alpha=0\); and the flow preserves \(\alpha\wedge d\alpha\). There \(d\alpha\) is defined from \(\alpha\) by the constant-field formula, not typed in. In ReebFlowExtDeriv.lean (kernel-checked 2026-09-21, same toolchain; eleven axiom reports on the three standard axioms, linter warnings only) it is proved equal to Mathlib's extDeriv of \(\alpha_\text{cat}\) as a Mathlib 1-form, with value \(-2r\,dr\wedge d\theta\), and \((\alpha\wedge d\alpha)(\partial_r,\partial_\theta,\partial_z)=-2r\); the wedge itself is still the explicit formula, not a Mathlib wedge. In ReebNoAttractor.lean (kernel-checked 2026-09-21, same toolchain; nine axiom reports on the three standard axioms) the step from volume preservation to no attracting set is verified in the elementary \((r,\theta,z)\)-chart model: \(\mu(U)\le\mu(A)\) for measure-preserving maps and a closed \(A\) with finite-measure thickening; translation preserves Lebesgue measure; a nonempty closed invariant set attracting a neighbourhood of itself is the whole space; there is no nonempty compact invariant set; and the unqualified claim is refuted (the whole space attracts itself). Still by hand: that the contact volume is a constant multiple of Lebesgue measure in the Cartesian chart, and that the model flow is the manifold's Reeb flow; no single theorem instantiates the general result at the Reeb flow. The Lean file defines the orbit as \((r_0,\theta_0,z_0+t)\) and proves its \(z\)-advance is \(t\,\alpha(R)\); that this curve is the integral curve of \(R\) is by inspection. But its integral curves \((r_0, \theta_0, z_0 + t)\) hold \(r\) and \(\theta\) fixed: they are straight vertical lines, not helices. And since the Reeb flow preserves \(\alpha\) (hence \(\alpha \wedge d\alpha\), the contact volume), it is volume-preserving and therefore cannot have an attracting set of smaller volume than its basin (so no attracting fixed point, periodic orbit or lower-dimensional attractor; the whole space trivially attracts itself, so the qualification is needed), for any choice of contact form on any contact manifold. Proof sketch (derived by hand): if a closed set \(A\) attracts every point of \(U\), the sets \(E_N=\{x\in U:\phi_n x\in A_\varepsilon\ \forall n\ge N\}\) increase to a set containing \(U\) and each has measure at most \(\mu(A_\varepsilon)\), so \(\mu(U)\le\mu(A)\). For this flow, translation in \(z\) and an isometry, more is true: a nonempty closed invariant set attracting a neighbourhood of itself is all of \(\mathbb{R}^3\), and there is no nonempty compact invariant set. (The flow of \(R\) is the case \(H\equiv-1\) of Gaset et al. 2020, Prop. 9, where \(X_{-1}=R\).) Dissipation on a contact manifold requires a contact Hamiltonian flow \(X_H\) with \(R(H)\neq0\): there \(\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\alpha=-R(H)\alpha\), so \(\mathcal{L}_{X_H}(\alpha\wedge d\alpha)=-2R(H)\,\alpha\wedge d\alpha\) (derived by hand). This paper does not derive its confinement from such a flow. (Corrected 2026-09-19; see Theorem 1.) The actual confinement claimed below comes from the DNLS equation's self-trapping transition, a separate and genuinely dissipative-free localization phenomenon (Eilbeck, Lomdahl & Scott 1985) — the contact manifold supplies coordinates, not the confining dynamics.

2.2 The generative operator pipeline

Define the pipeline \(G = U \circ F \circ K \circ C\), where the four operators act on a state \(\psi \in L^2(\mathcal{X}_\text{cat})\):

OperatorPhysical roleCatalytic interpretation
\(C\)CompressionAdsorption / pore entry
\(K\)Constrained pathTransition-state geometry; pore wall constraint
\(F\)FoldSelectivity filter; irreversible branching
\(U\)StabilisationProduct desorption; catalyst regeneration

2.3 The DNLS equation

On a lattice of \(N\) catalytic sites, the DNLS equation is:

\[ i\dot{\psi}_n = -J(\psi_{n+1} + \psi_{n-1}) - \lambda|\psi_n|^2\psi_n \]

where \(J > 0\) is inter-site coupling and \(\lambda > 0\) is the on-site binding energy. The Inverse Participation Ratio \(\text{IPR}(t) = \sum_n|\psi_n|^4 / (\sum_n|\psi_n|^2)^2\) measures localisation: IPR → 0 (delocalised) vs IPR → 1 (self-trapped).

3. Self-Trapping Selectivity Principle

Theorem 1 — Self-Trapping Selectivity · CatGT

Let \((\mathcal{X}_\text{cat}, \alpha_\text{cat})\) be the catalyst contact manifold with Reeb field \(R=\partial_z\), \(a\) a lattice-spacing length scale, and \(G = U \circ F \circ K \circ C\) the generative pipeline. Define the self-trapping tube \(\mathcal{T}_\lambda = \{(r,\theta,z) : r \leq r^*(\lambda)\}\), with \(r^*(\lambda) := a\sqrt{J/\lambda}\) — now a genuine length, not the dimensionless \(\sqrt{J/\lambda}\) of earlier drafts. Then:

(i) \(\mathcal{T}_\lambda\) is a coordinate region of \(\mathcal{X}_\text{cat}\), not a dynamical attractor: \(R\)'s flow preserves \(\alpha \wedge d\alpha\) and so has no attracting set of smaller volume than its basin, and its orbits are straight lines in \(z\), not helices. \(\mathcal{T}_\lambda\) is simply the set of points with \(r \leq r^*(\lambda)\).

(ii) MODEL A DNLS state whose radial coordinate satisfies the self-trapping condition remains confined to \(r(t) \leq r^*(\lambda)\) under the pipeline's (energy-conserving) dynamics — the discrete self-trapping transition of the DNLS equation itself is the confining mechanism, not the contact manifold's Reeb flow. How this connects to a fixed point of the discrete pipeline \(G\) is not established here; see the To-Do entry.

(iii) The transition-state selectivity is \(\sigma = 1 - (r^*(\lambda)/r_\text{pore})^2 = 1 - (J/\lambda)(a/r_\text{pore})^2\) — dimensionally consistent now that \(r^*(\lambda)\) carries units of length, unlike the earlier \(\sigma = 1 - J/(\lambda r_\text{pore}^2)\), which required \(J/\lambda\) to carry length² on its own.

Normalization of \(r^*\) — OPEN (added 2026-09-20)

The form \(r^*=a\sqrt{J/\lambda}\) is what a continuum sech soliton of fixed peak amplitude \(A\) gives: \(w=a\sqrt{2J/\lambda}/A\). The DNLS is usually studied at fixed conserved norm \(P=\sum|\psi_n|^2\), where the soliton width is \(w\simeq 4Ja/(\lambda P)\) — linear, not square-root, in \(J/\lambda\). SIMULATION scripts/soliton_width_check.py (400-site ring, \(J=1\), \(P=1\), energy minimisation at fixed norm): widths 9.977, 19.988, 40.171 at \(\lambda=0.4,0.2,0.1\) against \(4J/(\lambda P)=10,20,40\); fitted exponent \(d\ln w/d\ln\lambda=-1.005\) over \(\lambda\in[0.1,0.4]\) (a square-root law would give \(-0.5\)); \(-1.006\) against \(P\) at \(\lambda=0.1\). The \(\lambda=0.05\) point is finite-ring affected and excluded. Valid only for \(w\gg a\), i.e. \(J/\lambda\gg1\). The direction of the claim (larger \(\lambda\), smaller \(r^*\), more confinement) is the same either way; the quantitative form of \(r^*\), and so the numbers in \(\sigma\), depend on which normalization the physical problem realises, and that is not decided here.

Corollary 1 — Metal ensemble effects (Pt–Sn)

Corollary 1

On a bimetallic Pt–Sn surface, MODEL the promoter Sn reduces the size of contiguous Pt ensembles. In the model this constrains pathways to those requiring ≤ 2 adjacent Pt atoms, recovering the geometric ensemble effect — and, via Theorem 1(iii)'s \(\sigma=1-(r^*/r_\text{pore})^2\), correctly predicts selectivity increasing with Sn loading (Prediction 2), not decreasing as earlier drafts of that prediction had it.

4. Falsifiable Predictions

Prediction 1 — DNLS self-trapping threshold in zeolite pores

OPEN Reopened 2026-09-20. The formula stated in the 2026-09-19 revision, \(\lambda_c\approx J(\sigma_\text{LJ}/r_\text{pore})^2\), is withdrawn. Two problems. First, that revision contradicted itself: the formula increases as \(r_\text{pore}\) decreases, while the accompanying sentence said smaller pores need less nonlinearity. Second, its supporting argument read \(N\) in the Eilbeck–Lomdahl–Scott parameter \(\chi=\lambda N/J\) as a site count \(N\sim(r_\text{pore}/\sigma_\text{LJ})^2\). In the DNLS literature \(N\) there is the conserved norm \(\sum_j|A_j|^2\), not the number of sites. (Read from the Eilbeck–Johansson review, arXiv:nlin/0211049; the 1985 paper itself has not been re-checked.)

What the deposit does support, for a uniform state on an \(N\)-site ring at fixed norm \(P\), is a modulational-instability threshold that decreases with site count: \(\lambda_\text{MI}=NJ(1-\cos\tfrac{2\pi}{N})\approx 2\pi^2J/N\) — DERIVED from the Bogoliubov dispersion \(\omega_k^2=\epsilon_k(\epsilon_k-2\lambda\rho)\), \(\epsilon_k=2J(1-\cos k)\), \(\rho=P/N\), at \(k=2\pi/N\). SIMULATION scripts/mi_threshold_check.py brackets it: for \(N=8,16,32\) the peak density relative to uniform is 1.00 at \(0.7\lambda_\text{MI}\), and 7.10 / 5.02 / 4.69 at \(1.5\lambda_\text{MI}\), 6.95 / 13.32 / 7.55 at \(3\lambda_\text{MI}\), norm conserved to six digits. A bracket only; it does not locate the threshold more finely than the 0.7× to 1.5× interval. A separate energetic estimate for full localisation, \(\lambda P\gtrsim 4J\) (from \(w\simeq 4Ja/(\lambda P)\lesssim a\)), is independent of \(N\) at large \(N\) and was not simulated.

These are thresholds for two different notions of "self-trapped" on a 1D ring; neither has been mapped to a zeolite pore. That mapping needs an operational definition of \(\lambda_c\), a decision whether the relevant \(N\) is a site count or a norm, and a treatment of a two-dimensional pore cross-section (the literature reports a finite threshold in 2D that 1D does not have). A falsifiable version of this prediction needs those three choices first, then a molecular-dynamics comparison. The sign of the dependence of \(\lambda_c\) on pore size is not asserted.

Prediction 2 — Ensemble-radius scaling on Pt–Sn

Corrected 2026-09-19 — see the To-Do entry. The fraction of surviving size-2 Pt-only ensembles on \(\text{Pt}_{1-x}\text{Sn}_x\) — the geometric quantity driving the undesired hydrogenolysis/coking pathway — is proposed to scale as \((1-x)^2\) (pending resolution against the alternative form \(1-x^2\); see ensemble_scaling_forms_diverge below), matching the DNLS confinement fraction: \((1-x)^2 \approx (r^*/r_\text{pore})^2\). Propane dehydrogenation selectivity toward propylene is the complement of this survival fraction, \(\sigma = 1-(r^*/r_\text{pore})^2 \approx 1-(1-x)^2\) — increasing from 0 at \(x=0\) toward 1 as \(x\to1\), matching the empirically established effect of Sn addition raising propylene selectivity by breaking up large Pt ensembles. Earlier drafts equated \((1-x)^2\) directly with selectivity — backwards, since \((1-x)^2\) is a decreasing quantity (ensemble survival) while selectivity is its increasing complement. Testable via in-situ XAS measurements of average Pt ensemble size correlated against measured propylene selectivity.

/-- Critical (self-trapping) radius r*(λ) = a·√(J/λ), where `a > 0` is the
    lattice-spacing length scale. J and λ are both energies, so J/λ is
    dimensionless and `a` is what gives r* the dimension of a length
    (Theorem 1's 2026-09-19 correction on the paper's page). NOTE: this is
    the *definition* the theorems below are stated against; that r* is the
    physically correct DNLS self-trapping width is a separate claim, and
    depends on what is held fixed (see the 2026-09-20 note in the header). -/
noncomputable def criticalRadius (a J lam : ℝ) (ha : 0 < a) (hJ : 0 < J)
    (hlam : 0 < lam) : ℝ :=
  a * Real.sqrt (J / lam)

/-- **Self-Trapping Selectivity Principle** — formal core of Theorem 1.
    r² ≤ a²·(J/λ) ⟹ r ≤ r*(λ) = a·√(J/λ).
    NOTE (what this does and does not say): the hypothesis is the squared
    form of the conclusion, so this is the equivalence "r ≤ r* ⟸ r² ≤ r*²"
    for r ≥ 0, i.e. sqrt-monotonicity. It does NOT derive confinement from
    any DNLS dynamics; that is Theorem 1(ii)'s open content. -/
theorem helical_selectivity (a J lam : ℝ) (ha : 0 < a) (hJ : 0 < J)
    (hlam : 0 < lam) (r_state : ℝ) (hr : 0 ≤ r_state)
    (h_confined : r_state ^ 2 ≤ a ^ 2 * (J / lam)) :
    r_state ≤ criticalRadius a J lam ha hJ hlam := by
  unfold criticalRadius
  have h1 : r_state ≤ Real.sqrt (a ^ 2 * (J / lam)) := by
    rw [← Real.sqrt_sq hr]; exact Real.sqrt_le_sqrt h_confined
  have h2 : Real.sqrt (a ^ 2 * (J / lam)) = a * Real.sqrt (J / lam) := by
    rw [Real.sqrt_mul (sq_nonneg a), Real.sqrt_sq ha.le]
  rw [h2] at h1
  exact h1

5.1 Operator order: where non-commutativity actually lives

Correction (2026-06-18): earlier versions of the zeolite operator-order paper (v1, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.20563363, and v2, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.21296707) asserted that the aperture gate K fails to commute with a pointwise fold Fψ = ψ + λ|ψ|²ψ, via a boundary term ∝ δ(r − rap). That claim is false — the two commute exactly, for every state — and the three theorems below establish what is actually true. Order-dependence is carried by whichever operator transports amplitude between sites, never by a gate acting pointwise.

ZeoliteCommutation.lean — gate vs. pointwise fold vs. coupling ✓ CLOSED — sorry-free 
-- 3-site ring, real amplitudes (so |v|²v = v³).
def coupling (v : Fin 3 → ℝ) : Fin 3 → ℝ := ![v 1 + v 2, v 0 + v 2, v 0 + v 1]
def onsite   (v : Fin 3 → ℝ) : Fin 3 → ℝ := fun i => (v i) ^ 3
def gate     (v : Fin 3 → ℝ) : Fin 3 → ℝ := ![v 0, 0, v 2]
def psi      : Fin 3 → ℝ := ![1, 1, 0]

/-- The 0/1 aperture gate commutes with the pointwise fold — for EVERY state. -/
theorem gate_commutes (v : Fin 3 → ℝ) : gate (onsite v) = onsite (gate v)

/-- Inter-site coupling does NOT commute with the on-site fold (commutator −6). -/
theorem coupling_not_commute : coupling (onsite psi) ≠ onsite (coupling psi)

/-- The gate does NOT commute with the corrected fold F = coupling ∘ onsite. -/
theorem gate_fold_not_commute :
    gate (coupling (onsite psi)) ≠ coupling (onsite (gate psi))

6. Discussion

Relation to existing theory

This does not contradict DFT-based active-site design — it addresses a different question. DFT explains why a given site is reactive; it is silent on why identical acid sites in different pore geometries give different products. The Brønsted–Evans–Polanyi relation corresponds to the linearisation of \(K\) near \(x^*\) (that connection remains stated informally; \(x^*\) is not formally defined here — see the To-Do entry). The Thiele modulus is the ratio \(r_\text{pore}/r^*(\lambda)\).

Operator order and zeolite selectivity

The firing order of the compression/constraint/fold/unfold operators is not fixed but system-dependent. In ZSM-5, constraint fires before folding (the pore constrains first). In MCM-22, folding fires before constraint (the intermediate folds inside the supercage first). This operator-order switching under varied temperature, pressure, or concentration is a falsifiable DRIFTS prediction, independent of the self-trapping radius claim above.

Open questions

1. Quantitative fit against Sousa et al.'s measured selectivity, blocked on access to the data tables — see the To-Do entry. 2. Normalization of \(r^*(\lambda)\): fixed peak amplitude versus fixed norm (§3). 3. Prediction 1: an operational definition of \(\lambda_c\), the role of \(N\), and 2D pore cross-sections (§4). 4. Finite-size scaling of \(r^*(\lambda)\) for chain lengths \(N \in \{100, 500, 1000\}\). 5. Definition of \(C,K,F,U\) and \(x^*\) on \(L^2(\mathcal{X}_\text{cat})\).
